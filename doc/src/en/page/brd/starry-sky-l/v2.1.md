The onboard resources of the FPGA SoM are as follows:

- Main chip: XC7Z020CLG484-1I
    - ARM specification: Cortex-A9 dual-core, 667 MHz
    - FPGA specification:
        - Architecture: Artix7
        - Logic cells: 85K

        - Speed grade: -1

- Crystal oscillator: one 33.3333 MHz active crystal oscillator connected to the PS interface
- Four DF40HC-100DP board-to-board connectors
    - 54 PS MIO signals are brought out
    - 200 PL signals are brought out (96 differential pairs)

The main design features of the StarrySky v2.1 board are as follows:

- The StarrySky development board measures **110X84 mm**, about **twice** the area of a standard credit card.
- The power network has been redesigned, increasing the current supply capability from 1.2 A to 2 A, supporting an external **5 V power adapter**, and supporting multiple power rails.
- The FPGA uses a SoM design with four BTB connectors. The hardware specification is: **XC7Z020CLG484 + 1 GB DDR3L + 8 GB eMMC**.
- The board has an onboard NOR Flash programmer **(HFPLink)** that implements drag-and-drop programming and supports firmware upgrades **(through an ISP that supports the USB protocol)**.
- Multiple **analog switches** are used to implement **IO multiplexing** of the PL-side ports of the FPGA, so that as many peripherals as possible can be attached, and all IO on the FPGA SoM is brought out.
- The processor core switching and frequency selection switches are separated in the design, and **active-high enable logic** is used to make DIP switch setting convenient.
- Geometric constraints were fully considered, and low-actuation-force devices **(160 gf)** were selected for the buttons and switches to improve the hardware operating experience.
- **Silkscreens and labels are provided on the board for the DIP switches and important interfaces**, making the board easier for students to use.

The PS-side BANKs of the FPGA SoM include BANK502, BANK500, and BANK501. BANK502 uses a 1.5 V I/O standard and is used to connect two DDR3 chips. BANK500 uses a 3.3 V I/O standard and is used to connect peripherals that comply with the 3.3 V electrical standard, such as UART, LEDs, and buttons. BANK501 uses a 1.8 V I/O standard and is used to connect peripherals that comply with the 1.8 V electrical standard, such as USB HOST and SDIO. The PL-side BANKs include BANK13, BANK33, BANK34, and BANK35. The I/O standard of all four PL BANKs is **1.8 V**, and they are used to connect common SoC peripherals such as UART, I2C, SPI, and GPIO.

!!! warning "The FPGA PL-Side I/O Standard Is 1.8 V"
    - **All BANKs on the PL side of the FPGA SoM use a 1.8 V I/O standard**, so if the PL-side expansion port is used, **level shifter** chips are required for level and protocol conversion.
    - When selecting level shifter chips, pay attention to parameters such as conversion direction, maximum data rate, and drive capability. All onboard peripherals on the StarrySky board that do not use the 1.8 V I/O standard have already been converted using level shifter chips.

!!! danger "Power Supply Load Notes"
    - At present, the current load of the StarrySky development board when running a program normally is about **400–450 mA**. Drawing power from a standard USB port on a laptop or desktop generally meets the supply requirements, and **no additional power supply is needed**. Of course, to meet certain specific requirements, the StarrySky development board also supports power from an external 5 V/2 A power adapter through the Type-C interface, but the project team does not include this adapter in the accessories. Students who need one must prepare it themselves.
    - A power adapter is usually connected directly to the mains, and its voltage and current are relatively high, so there is a certain danger. **If students really need to use a power adapter to power the board, please select an adapter that meets safety standards and pay attention to electrical safety**.

!!! warning "Debugging Precautions"
    Before actually operating the board, students should carefully read and confirm each of the precautions below. In particular, students who have no previous embedded debugging experience must follow these precautions:

    - Make sure your hands are dry before touching the board and other materials.
    - Before touching the board, touch a metal object first to discharge any static electricity that may have built up on your body.
    - When handling the board, hold it by the edges of the circuit board to avoid touching the chips with your hands as much as possible, so that excess static electricity from your body is not conducted to the chip pins.
    - When using the Type-C cable for power, give priority to the PC's USB 3.0 port to ensure power supply stability as much as possible.
    - The board has power selection switches, slide switches, and DIP switches. Before powering on, confirm that they are all correctly set to one side rather than the middle position **(dead zone)**. In addition, try to ensure that no switch is toggled while the board is powered.
    - Do not connect or disconnect the SoC board and the FPGA SoM while they are powered, to prevent latch-up caused by incorrect hot-plugging from damaging the chips.
    - Once power is applied, if you observe smoke, an abnormal odor, electrical arcing or noise, components becoming hot, or other abnormal phenomena—especially a **buzzing acoustic noise**—**cut off the power immediately**.

!!! info "Acoustic Noise"
    Acoustic noise is often divided into inductor whine and capacitor whine. Inductor whine is the most common. It is mostly caused by incorrectly selected component parameters, unstable loads, or light-load/overload conditions. **Load instability** is often the main cause, which means there may be a short circuit somewhere on the board.

#### Hardware Operations
First, students need to take the board out of the **grid anti-static bag**. At present, the One Student One Chip (YSYX) SoC **implements its memory access path by accessing the DDR3 chip on the FPGA side through the ChipLink interface**, so an FPGA SoM is required for RT-Thread to boot correctly:

![](../../../res/img/brd/starry-sky-l/v2.1/core/datapath.webp)
/// caption
Three data paths supported by the YSYS SoC
///

In fact, the FPGA SoM has already been inserted into the back of the board in advance; you only need to confirm it.

!!! info "Installing/Removing the FPGA SoM"
    The FPGA SoM used with this SoC is a [SoM (System on Module)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/System_on_module). The SoM is connected to the SoC baseboard through four **high-speed BTB connectors** of model DF40C-100DS. This type of connector is commonly used in applications requiring stable high-frequency transmission. By default, the FPGA SoM is already inserted into the connectors, and **generally it does not need to be removed**. If it is necessary to install or remove the SoM, simply align it with the connectors and press it in or pull it out vertically.

After confirming that the FPGA SoM is correctly inserted, **three micro DIP switches** must also be set in hardware for the fourth-generation YSYS SoC to work normally. Specifically, three steps are required:

- Set the frequency of the PLL output clock
- Set the output frequency of the clock tree
- Enable the processor core corresponding to a specific student ID

First, a brief introduction to the RCG **(global clock and reset module)** of the fourth-generation YSYS SoC. The functional block diagram of this module is shown below:

![](../../../res/img/brd/starry-sky-l/v2.1/core/rcg_intro.webp)
/// caption
RCG module of the fourth-generation YSYS SoC
///

As can be seen from the figure above, the fourth-generation YSYS SoC has three clock domains: the **processor core clock domain (core_clk)**, the **high-speed peripheral clock domain (hs_clk)**, and the **low-speed peripheral clock domain (ls_clk)**. The high-speed peripheral clock domain is fixed at 100 MHz, and the low-speed clock domain is fixed at 25 MHz. The processor core clock is set to different frequencies according to the values of `pll_cfg[2:0]` and `clk_cfg[6:0]`. The entire clock source of the fourth-generation YSYS SoC is provided by an external active crystal oscillator. The design supports external crystal oscillator inputs of 25 MHz and 100 MHz, where 25 MHz is the clock input source in normal operating mode and 100 MHz is the bypass clock input source when the PLL is not working properly. In addition, each clock domain uses a two-stage synchronizer to generate a stable reset release signal.

The first step of DIP switch setup is to set the **PLL output clock frequency selection bits**, which correspond to `pll_cfg[2:0]` in the red box in the upper-left corner of the figure above and are used to set the PLL output clock frequency. The corresponding DIP switch bits need to be set to `ON`. The second step of DIP switch setup is to set the **clock tree output frequency**, which corresponds to `clk_cfg[6:0]` in the lower-middle part of the figure above and is used to set the processor core operating clock frequency. The correspondence between the specific settings of `pll_cfg[2:0]` and `clk_cfg[6:0]` and the SoC output clock frequencies is shown in the table below:

| pll_cfg | clk_cfg | Crystal input frequency | PLL output frequency | Processor core clock frequency (core_clk) | High-speed clock domain frequency (hs_clk) | Low-speed clock domain frequency (ls_clk) |
| :-: | :-: | :-: | :-: | :-: | :-: | :-: |
| 3'b001 | 7'b0100010 | 25MHz  | 100MHz | 25MHz  | 100MHz | 25MHz |
| 3'b001 | 7'b0100001 | 25MHz  | 100MHz | 50MHz  | 100MHz | 25MHz |
| 3'b001 | 7'b0100000 | 25MHz  | 100MHz | 100MHz | 100MHz | 25MHz |
| 3'b101 | 7'b0100101 | 25MHz  | 300MHz | 150MHz | 100MHz | 25MHz |
| 3'b011 | 7'b0101000 | 25MHz  | 200MHz | 200MHz | 100MHz | 25MHz |
| 3'b101 | 7'b0100100 | 25MHz  | 300MHz | 300MHz | 100MHz | 25MHz |
| 3'b111 | 7'b0110000 | 25MHz  | 400MHz | 400MHz | 100MHz | 25MHz |
| 3'b000 | 7'b1000000 | 100MHz | none   | 100MHz | 100MHz | 25MHz |
| 3'b000 | 7'b1000001 | 100MHz | none   | 50MHz  | 100MHz | 25MHz |
| 3'b000 | 7'b1000010 | 100MHz | none   | 25MHz  | 100MHz | 25MHz |

For example, when `pll_cfg[3:0] = 3'b111 clk_cfg[6:0] = 7'b0110000` is set, the SoC uses the 25 MHz external crystal oscillator as the clock input source, the processor core clock is 400 MHz, the high-speed clock domain is 100 MHz, and the low-speed clock domain is 25 MHz.

!!! warning "100 MHz External Crystal Oscillator Clock Input"
    Note that the 100 MHz external crystal oscillator clock source is an alternative clock source. At present, only the 25 MHz active crystal oscillator is populated on the board, so the frequencies in the last three rows of the table above cannot be set correctly.

Now the correspondence between the DIP switch positions and the signals on the SoC is introduced, along with the function definitions of each position on the board's **micro DIP switches**. As can be seen from the figure below, there are three micro DIP switches on the board. The topmost DIP switch has 4 positions and is used to **set the clock output state**; its lower 3 positions correspond to `pll_cfg[2:0]` described earlier. The DIP switch on the lower right has 8 positions and is used to **set the clock tree output frequency**; its lower 7 positions correspond to `clk_cfg[6:0]` described earlier. The DIP switch on the lower left has 6 positions and is used to **set the core selection state**:

![](../../../res/img/brd/starry-sky-l/v2.1/perip/dip_switch_1.webp)
/// caption
Function definitions of the DIP switch positions
///

Function definitions of the 4-position DIP switch **(from left to right)**:

- Undefined **(1 position)**
- PLL output clock frequency selection bits **(3 positions)**

Function definitions of the 8-position DIP switch **(from left to right)**:

- Undefined **(1 position)**
- Clock tree configuration bits **(7 positions)**

Function definitions of the 6-position DIP switch **(from left to right)**:

- Undefined **(2 positions)**
- Processor core selection bits **(4 positions)**

!!! warning "Core Selection DIP Switch Notes"
    - The highest position **(the first from the left)** of the 4-position DIP switch has no defined function.
    - The highest two positions **(from the left)** of the 6-position DIP switch have no defined function.
    - The highest position **(the first from the left)** of the 8-position DIP switch has no defined function.

!!! tip "Clock DIP Switch Settings"
    - During testing, it is recommended to **first set the processor core clock frequency to 25 MHz and start testing from a low frequency**.
    - All clock output DIP switches use **active-high logic**, that is, when the switch is toggled toward the **ON** silkscreen mark on the package, it represents **logic 1**; otherwise it represents **logic 0**.

!!! tip "Tip"
    - The mechanical DIP switches on the board are 1.27 mm pitch micro DIP switches. Each position of this switch is relatively small and difficult to toggle directly by hand. You can use tweezers, a paper clip, or another pointed object. **Please use pointed objects safely**.
    - To make it convenient to measure the processor core clock frequency, the board provides a **core clock divided-by-four test point** and **external 25 MHz and 100 MHz crystal oscillator clock test points (the 100 MHz oscillator is not used)**:

    ![](../../../res/img/brd/starry-sky-l/v2.1/perip/osc_tp.webp)
    /// caption
    Clock frequency test points
    ///

    The **external crystal oscillator clock test points** can be used to test whether the external active crystal oscillator starts correctly, and the **core clock divided-by-four test point** can be used to confirm the core clock operating frequency. By measuring the frequency output at this test point under different DIP switch settings, the clock operating state of the processor core can be determined. **Note that this measured value is one quarter of the processor core clock**.

!!! danger "DIP Switch Operation"
    - Like slide switches, DIP switches must be correctly set to one side before power-on rather than the middle position **(mechanical dead zone)**, to prevent the SoC from sampling an unstable DIP switch level.

#### Installing Serial Debugging Software
Once the computer can successfully recognize the serial port number, serial debugging software is still needed to open the serial port and communicate with the board. ### Hardware Design
This section describes the hardware design of StarrySky v2.1 in detail. First, a brief summary of the main hardware design features of the v2.1 board:

- Designed with Cadence Orcad and Allegro; design time: **three weeks**; board mechanical dimensions: **11.18x9.59 cm**; board thickness: **1.6 mm**
    - **Eight-layer** stackup: **(TOP-GND02-ART03-PWR04-GND05-ART06-GND07-BOT)**
    - ENIG surface finish and **via-in-pad + resin-plugged vias** process; four-wire low-resistance testing performed
    - Outer-layer copper thickness **1** oz, inner-layer copper thickness **0.5** oz, ENIG thickness **1u''**
- Board length matching:
    - ChipLink: **0/50 mil** intra-group matching referenced to **tx_clk** and **rx_clk**
    - Two SDIO interfaces: **0/30 mil** intra-group matching referenced to clk
    - USB2.0: **0/50 mil** intra-group matching referenced to clk
    - Five USB differential pairs: **0/25 mil** intra-pair matching
    - JTAG signals: **0/25 mil** intra-pair matching
    - AUDIO signals: **0/25 mil** intra-pair matching
    - SDRAM signals: **0/250 mil** intra-pair matching
    - 24 PL-side differential IO pairs on the expansion port: **0/25 mil** intra-pair matching
- VGA and all crystal oscillator clock outputs have **ground shielding**, are kept away from high-frequency and analog signals, and all reference a complete ground plane.
- Analog ground has **single-point isolation**. All chips have complete power filtering. The power network uses copper pours for connections to ensure stable power supply, and a sufficient number of **ground return vias** are provided.

#### Power Network
The power network topology of the SoC board is shown below:

![](../../../res/img/brd/starry-sky-l/v2.1/core/power_tp.webp)
/// caption
Board power network topology
///

As can be seen from the figure above, the entire board is powered uniformly from a 5 V supply. To control peripheral power flexibly, three power inputs are provided on the board:

- The 5 V power from the SoC USB serial port
- The 5 V power from the FPGA PS-side USB serial port
- The 5 V DC power from an external USB power adapter

The board uses a **power selection switch** to select one of the three inputs to supply the board. After passing through the **latching power switch**, the 5 V supply is stepped down by three LDO chips to 3.3 V, 1.8 V, and 0.9 V for the various SoC and FPGA peripherals. The schematic is as follows:

![](../../../res/img/brd/starry-sky-l/v2.1/sch/sch_pwr_sck.webp)
/// caption
Power input interfaces
///

Here, USB_PWR, PS_PWR, and DC_PWR are three standard Type-C receptacles. USB_PWR exposes the power network **`5V_VBUS1`** and the differential input signals **`MAIN_DP/DN`** required by the SoC serial port. PS_PWR exposes the power network **`5V_VBUS2`** and the differential input signals **`PS_UART_DP/DN`** required by the FPGA PS-side serial port. Because DC_PWR is used only for power supply, only the power network **`5V_DC`** is brought out on its connector.

!!! info "Purpose of the 5.1 K Pull-Down Resistor on the Type-C Receptacle CC Pins"
    The CC pin on Type-C is the [Configuration Channel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USB-C). It is used to detect cable insertion, identify cable orientation, configure the operating mode **(current, PD mode)**, and negotiate the DFP **(downstream-facing port, host side)** and UFP **(upstream-facing port, device side)** roles. Relative to the host PC, the board's Type-C interface acts as a device-side interface, that is, it has the UFP role. The protocol specifies that the **`CC1`** and **`CC2`** pins of a UFP both require a pull-down resistor **`Rd=5.1KΩ`**.

The three power inputs described above pass through two [single-pole double-throw (SPDT) slide switches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Switch#Contact_terminology), PWR-SEL1 and PWR-SEL2. By combining the positions of these two switches, one power input can be selected to supply the **latching power switch PWR-LOCK**. PWR-LOCK is a [double-pole double-throw (DPDT) slide switch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Switch#Contact_terminology), and only one side of the switch is used here. When the latching switch is not locked down, it connects pins **`2`** and **`1`**, and the power network is not connected. When the latching power switch is pressed down and locked, PWR-LOCK connects pins **`2`** and **`3`**, allowing one power input to pass through the latching switch into the downstream power network. The power then passes through two protection devices, the bidirectional TVS diode SMAJ5.0CA and a 2 A fuse, and is filtered by multiple capacitors to finally produce a stable 5 V digital supply **`5V_IN`**:

![](../../../res/img/brd/starry-sky-l/v2.1/sch/sch_pwr_sel.webp)
/// caption
Power selection switches
///

The **`5V_IN`** supply then enters three RT2515H LDO chips and is stepped down to 3.3 V, 1.8 V, and 0.9 V. The RT2515H is a high-performance voltage regulator that supports a minimum input-to-output dropout of 200 mV and a maximum output current of 2 A. Different output voltages can be produced by configuring the resistance on its **`ADJ`** pin. In addition, the 3.3 V power network **`VCC_3V3`** is connected to a red LED to indicate whether the power circuit is operating properly:

![](../../../res/img/brd/starry-sky-l/v2.1/sch/sch_pwr_ldo.webp)
/// caption
Power conversion circuit
///

#### SoC Onboard Programmer (HFPLink) {#soc-hfplink}
The SoC section of the board includes a NOR Flash programmer called **HFPLink**. **HFPLink** is a Flash programmer designed by the project team. **HFP** in the name stands for **High-speed QSPI-Flash Programmer**. Because it also supports drag-and-drop programming like **DAPLink**, the suffix **Link** was added following the DAPLink naming style, forming the name **HFPLink**. The core principle of HFPLink's drag-and-drop programming is to use the USB device communication function of the CH32V103 main controller to emulate a USB removable device in software **(the USB data-transfer-only protocol)**, and then **redirect** the host PC's copy of a bin file to the USB removable device into operations that write data sectors to the NOR Flash. Many details must be handled, such as emulating a FAT16 file system and dividing sector data. The schematic is shown below:

![](../../../res/img/brd/starry-sky-l/v2.1/sch/sch_hfplink.webp)
/// caption

///

The HFPLink schematic is essentially the CH32V103 minimum system with an additional NOR Flash **(SPI protocol)** and USB interface. The core functionality is actually implemented in software.

#### SoC Function Switch {#soc_2}
The SoC function switch is designed so that the SoC USB-to-serial port and HFPLink can share one Type-C interface and switch the operating state of the NOR Flash at the same time. This function is controlled by an SPDT slide switch named HFP-SW. When HFP-SW is toggled so that pins **`1`** and **`2`** are connected, the **`PROG_SEL`** net is low, so pin **`6`** of every analog switch WAS3157B is low, connecting the signal nets on pins **`1`** and **`4`** of each analog switch. When HFP-SW is toggled so that pins **`3`** and **`2`** are connected, the **`PROG_SEL`** net is high, so pin **`6`** of every analog switch WAS3157B is high, connecting the signal nets on pins **`3`** and **`4`** of each analog switch:

![](../../../res/img/brd/starry-sky-l/v2.1/sch/sch_soc_func_sw.webp)
/// caption
SoC function switch
///

This circuit is possible because **the HFPLink and the SoC USB-to-serial port never operate at the same time**, so the differential signals **`MAIN_DP/DN`** on one Type-C interface can be reused. Moreover, when HFPLink programs the NOR Flash, the SPI signal lines connecting the SoC to the NOR Flash must be disconnected. Conversely, when the SoC needs to fetch and execute instructions from the NOR Flash, the SPI signal lines connecting HFPLink to the NOR Flash must be disconnected. That is, the connection state of six signal lines—two differential signals plus four SPI signals—must be **switched synchronously** between the two states: HFPLink operating and the SoC operating normally.

!!! info "Resistor Network Value Calculation"
    The VGA standard specifies that the `VGA_R`, `VGA_G`, and `VGA_B` pins carry analog signals in the range `0~0.714V`. For each analog signal, `0V` indicates no color and `0.714V` indicates full color. Because RGB444 mode is used, each channel has 2^4=16 color levels. For example, for the `VGA_R` channel, let the value of the combined input signals VGA_R3~VGA_R0 be $VGA_R[3:0]$, and let the voltage of the `VGA_R` net be $V_{R}$. To display the `VGA_R` channel color correctly, the following must hold:

    - When $VGA_R[3:0]=4'b0000$, $V_{R}=0V$
    - When $VGA_R[3:0]=4'b1111$, $V_{R}<=0.714V$
    - When $VGA_R[3:0]$ takes any value in $[4'b0000, 4'b1111]$, for any two values $VGA_R[3:0]_1 <= VGA_R[3:0]_2$, $V_{R1}<=V_{R2}$ must hold

    It follows that the resistor weights on VGA_R3~VGA_R0 are different. The specific resistance values can be calculated with circuit simulation software, such as the free [MultisimLive](https://www.multisim.com/):

    ![](../../../res/img/brd/starry-sky-l/v2.1/sch/vga_dac_sim.webp)
    /// caption
    MultisimLive simulation of the VGA resistor network
    ///

    The free version of **MultisimLive** limits the size of the circuits that can be built, but it is entirely sufficient for the resistor network value calculation here.

!!! info "Why the Xilinx ZYNQ Series Was Selected for the FPGA"
    - There are more Xilinx ZYNQ series development boards on the market than boards using other FPGA models, making it easier to find reference hardware and software documentation.
    - The PS-side peripheral controllers of ZYNQ can be routed to PL-side IO through EMIO, making it convenient to compare and verify them against IP functions developed by the project team.

#### ChipLink Hardware System

!!! info "ChipLink Preprogramming"
    Before the FPGA SoM was distributed to students, the ChipLink hardware system described in this section had already been programmed into the FPGA's onboard Flash. Students can use it directly and do not need to compile and download it themselves.

ChipLink is an inter-chip low-speed bus communication protocol **(between the FPGA and SoC)** proposed by SiFive. It is mainly used to fragment and reassemble the AXI4 requests sent by the SoC so that hardware logic resources on the FPGA can be accessed reliably. The Verilog code for the ChipLink controller on this FPGA SoM was generated by Chisel and passed simulation in VCS. In the specific implementation, `ChiplinkTop` and all its submodules used in the VCS simulation were imported into one file and renamed `ChiplinkTop.v`. Because many signals inside the `ChiplinkTop` instantiated during VCS simulation are floating or tied to constant values, the project team wrapped another layer, `chiplink_ctrl`, around `ChiplinkTop`. This module instantiates `ChiplinkTop`, ties the corresponding ports to constants, and was finally named `chiplink_ctrl.v`. In addition, because the ZYNQ DDR address mapping differs from the SoC memory access address range, the memory access addresses must be remapped. The ZYNQ memory address map is shown below:

![](../../../res/img/brd/starry-sky-l/v2.1/fpga/chiplink_addr_map.webp)
/// caption
ZYNQ OCM/DDR address map
///

Here, OCM stands for **On-Chip-Memory**. It is built into the ZYNQ chip and can serve as low-latency shared memory between the two PS-side CPU cores; it is also the memory in which the dual-core CPU executes applications. **Therefore, it is necessary to ensure that the memory access addresses of the fourth-generation YSYS SoC are correctly mapped to the DDR space. Otherwise, if they are remapped into OCM space, the program running on the PS side may be overwritten**. As can be seen from the figure above, ZYNQ supports a 1 GB address range from `0x0010_0000 - 0x3FFF_FFF`, but the ZYNQ SoM provided to students has only 512 MB of onboard DDR3. To simplify the implementation, the memory access requests of the fourth-generation YSYS SoC with a starting address of `0x8000_0000` are **remapped** to `0x0010_0000`. In this way, it is sufficient to ensure that when the PS-side CPU needs to access DDR, its memory access addresses do not overlap with those of the SoC. The remapping code is as follows:

```verilog
assign io_axi4_0_awaddr = aw_addr - 32'h8000_0000 + 32'h0010_0000;
assign io_axi4_0_araddr = ar_addr - 32'h8000_0000 + 32'h0010_0000;
assign chiplink_cpu_int = 1'b0;
```

Adding `chiplink_ctrl` to the **Block Design** and connecting it to the ZYNQ system completes the hardware design. The final hardware system is shown below:

![](../../../res/img/brd/starry-sky-l/v2.1/fpga/chiplink_bd.webp)
/// caption
ChipLink hardware system Block Design
///

The **ZYNQ Processing System** enables the AXI HP slave interface, the `FCLK_CLK0` and `FCLK_CLK1` clock outputs, and the `FCLK_RESET0_N` reset output. `FCLK_CLK0` is set to 25 MHz and is used as the input clock for `chiplink_ctrl`. `FCLK_CLK1` is set to 100 MHz and is used by the **AXI Interconnect** for AXI master communication. The other two clocks are each connected to their own **Processor System Reset** to generate reset signals.

Next, the pins of the `chiplink_ctrl` module must be constrained. Because `chiplink_ctrl` has a relatively large number of pins, they must be checked and confirmed carefully. In addition, the fourth-generation YSYS SoC uses a 28 nm process, so the pin I/O standard must be constrained to LVCMOS18. Finally, clock constraints must be applied to the `chiplink_ctrl` pins. The specific constraints are shown below:

![](../../../res/img/brd/starry-sky-l/v2.1/fpga/chiplink_contr.webp)
/// caption
ChipLink timing constraints
///

Vivado is then used for synthesis and implementation. After implementation, Vivado reported three timing-related Critical Warnings. After consulting Xilinx's **UG906** manual and analyzing the design code, the project team concluded that these three warnings are mainly caused by **the clock source generated at the PS-side MCM/PLL having no common path defined on the PL side**. This has no impact on the design and can be ignored.

![](../../../res/img/brd/starry-sky-l/v2.1/fpga/chiplink_log.webp)

After confirming that the post-implementation warnings can be ignored, generate the bitstream file, export the hardware to Vitis IDE, and create an application project named `Hello World` based on the exported hardware platform. The source code of the `Hello World` project is shown below:

```c
#include <stdio.h>
#include "platform.h"
#include "xil printf.h"
int main()
{
  init_platform();
  print("Hello World\n\r");
  print("Successfully ran Hello World application");
  printf("main addr: %p\n", main);
  while(1);
  cleanup_platform();
  return 0;
}
```

This `Hello World` test program is mainly used to initialize the PS-side system, provide reset/clock signals to the PL side, and perform a serial print test. Finally, it loops at `while(1)` waiting to receive DDR access requests sent from the PL side.
