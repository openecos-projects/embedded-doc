### Board Introduction

The StarrySky development board v1.2 uses a design consisting of an SoC baseboard plus an FPGA system-on-module (SoM). The FPGA SoM uses the ZYNQ7010 chip. The onboard resources of the SoC baseboard are shown in the figure below:

![](../../../res/img/brd/starry-sky-l/v1.2/core/board_func_color.webp)
/// caption
Onboard resources of the SoC baseboard
///

As can be seen from the figure above, the peripherals and interfaces of the SoC part are basically distributed on the lower-middle side of the board **(purple)**, while the PL and PS peripherals of the FPGA part are mainly distributed on the upper side and both edges of the board **(green and blue)**. Detailed descriptions of the peripherals and interfaces are given below:

- SoC
    - 1 active crystal oscillator socket
    - 1 TF card socket in SPI mode
    - 1 onboard programmer (HFPLink)
    - 1 Flash socket with a built-in NOR Flash chip; after the SoC resets, it fetches instructions from this Flash chip
    - 1 UART interface, using the CP2102 chip
    - 1 external 5 V DC power input, using a Type-C interface
    - 2 power mode selection switches
    - 1 latching power switch
    - 2 DIP switches, used to set the PLL clock output and core selection
- System
    - 1 red power indicator LED
    - 1 PS function reset button, 1 PS debug reset button
    - 1 FPGA boot mode selection switch
    - 1 FPGA JTAG debug interface
    - 1 FPGA peripheral function selection switch
- PS-side peripherals
    - 1 UART interface, using the CP2102 chip
    - 1 SDIO interface; the SD card can boot PetaLinux, and the eMMC can store programs persistently
    - 1 SDIO WiFi, using the AP6212 module, with independent power control
    - 1 CAN interface, using the TJA1050 high-speed transceiver chip
    - 1 USB HOST, using the USB3320C chip
    - 1 6-pin PMOD expansion port
    - 2 user-defined buttons
    - 2 user-defined blue LEDs
    - 4 WS2812C RGB LEDs
- PL-side peripherals
    - 1 VGA interface, using a resistor network to implement the DAC
    - 1 PS/2 interface, for connecting a PS/2-compatible keyboard
    - 1 RTC clock, using the PCF8563 chip
    - 1 EEPROM, using the AT24C64 chip
    - 1 SPI Flash, using the W25Q128JVSIQ chip
    - 1 I2S interface, using the WM8960 chip, supporting audio input/output and MIC recording
    - 1 user-defined button
    - 1 user-defined blue LED
    - 1 general-purpose IO, often used for clock frequency output
    - 4 WS2812C RGB LEDs
- 1 SO-DIMM 204P interface, used to connect the ZYNQ7010/7020 FPGA SoM

The FPGA SoM is shown in the figures below:

![](../../../res/img/brd/starry-sky-l/v1.2/fpga/som_black_1.webp)
/// caption

///

![](../../../res/img/brd/starry-sky-l/v1.2/fpga/som_black_2.webp)

The onboard resources of the FPGA SoM are as follows:

- Main chip: XC7Z010CLG400
- DDR3: MT41K128M16JT-125 x2 **(512MB)**
- eMMC: KLM8G1GETF-B041 **(8GB)**
- Flash: W25Q128JVSQ **(16MB)**

The main design features of the StarrySky v1.2 board are as follows:

- The StarrySky development board measures **110 x 84 mm**, about **twice** the area of a standard credit card.
- The power network was redesigned, increasing the power supply capability from 1.2 A to 2 A, supporting an external **5 V power adapter**, and supporting multi-rail power management.
- The FPGA uses a SO-DIMM 204P SoM design, with hardware specifications: **XC7Z010-1CLG400C + 512MB DDR3 + 8GB eMMC**.
- Onboard NOR Flash programmer **(HFPLink)** implementing drag-and-drop programming; the programmer supports firmware upgrades **(via ISP supporting the USB protocol)**.
- Multiple **analog switches** are used to implement **IO multiplexing** of the FPGA-side PL ports, so as to attach as many peripherals as possible and break out all IO on the FPGA SoM.
- In the design, the processor core switching and frequency selection switches are separated and use **active-high selection logic**, making DIP switch setting convenient.
- Geometric constraints were fully considered; the buttons and switches use low actuation force components **(160 gf)** to improve the hardware operating experience.
- **Silk-screen labels and markings are provided for the DIP switches and important interfaces on the board**, making them convenient for students to use.

The hardware resource block diagram of the StarrySky v1.2 board is shown below:

![](../../../res/img/brd/starry-sky-l/v1.2/core/board_res.webp)
/// caption
Hardware resources
///

The BANKs on the PS side of the FPGA SoM are BANK502, BANK500, and BANK501. BANK502 has a level standard of 1.5 V and is used to connect two DDR3 chips. BANK500 has a level standard of 3.3 V and is used to connect peripherals compliant with the 3.3 V electrical standard, such as UART, LEDs, and buttons. BANK501 has a level standard of 1.8 V and is used to connect peripherals compliant with the 1.8 V electrical standard, such as USB HOST and I2S.

!!! danger "Power supply load notes"
    - Currently, the StarrySky development board draws about **400~450 mA** when running programs normally. Powering it from a standard USB port on a laptop or desktop generally meets the supply requirement, and **no additional power supply is needed**. Of course, to meet certain specific needs, the StarrySky development board also supports power from an external 5V/2A power adapter via the Type-C interface, but the project team does not include such an adapter in the accessories; students who need it must prepare one themselves.
    - Power adapters are generally connected directly to the mains, with relatively high voltage and current, and pose certain hazards. **If you really need to use a power adapter to power the board, please choose an adapter that meets safety standards and pay attention to electrical safety**.

### Power-On Test

This section mainly describes how to perform basic hardware and software tests on the board. To make testing convenient for students, **a NOR Flash chip pre-programmed with the RT-Thread program is already installed in the Flash socket on the board**. In addition, before the board was shipped, the project team ran this test on the cores on the SoC, so students only need to follow the sequence described below to run the RT-Thread program. The specific steps of the power-on test are shown in the figure below; they are mainly divided into two parts: **hardware operations** and **software setup**:

![](../../../res/img/brd/starry-sky-l/v1.2/core/test_flow.webp)
/// caption
Power-on test steps
///

The yellow boxes are pure hardware operations, while the blue boxes involve software operations.

!!! warning "Debugging notes"
    Please read carefully and confirm each of the following notes before actually operating the board. In particular, students without prior embedded debugging experience must follow these notes:

    - Confirm that your hands are dry before touching the board and other materials.
    - Before touching the board, touch a metal object first to discharge any static electricity that may have built up on your body.
    - When handling the board, hold it by the edges of the PCB, so as to avoid touching the chips with your hands as much as possible and prevent excess static electricity from your body from being conducted to the chip pins.
    - When powering via the Type-C cable, prefer the PC's USB 3.0 port to ensure stable power supply as much as possible.
    - There are power selection switches, slide switches, and DIP switches on the board. Before powering on, confirm that all of them are properly toggled to one side rather than the middle position **(dead zone)**. In addition, try to ensure that no switch is toggled while the board is powered.
    - Do not plug or unplug the SoC board and the FPGA SoM while powered, to prevent latch-up caused by such improper hot-plugging operations and damage to the chips.
    - Once power is applied, if you observe any abnormal phenomena such as smoke, unusual odor, sound or light from electrical discharge, or components getting hot, especially when you hear a **"buzzing/zizzing" noise (coil whine)**, **cut off the power immediately**.

!!! info "Acoustic noise (coil whine)"
    Acoustic noise is often divided into inductor whine and capacitor whine. Inductor whine is the most common; it is mostly caused by improperly selected component parameters, unstable load, or light-load/overload conditions, and **unstable load** is often the main cause, which means there may be a short circuit somewhere on the board.


#### Hardware Operations

First, students need to take the board out of the **mesh anti-static bag**. Currently, the One Student One Chip (YSYX) SoC **accesses memory through the ChipLink interface to the DDR3 chips on the FPGA side**, so an FPGA SoM is required for RT-Thread to boot correctly:

![](../../../res/img/brd/starry-sky-l/v1.2/core/datapath.webp)
/// caption
Three data paths supported by the One Student One Chip (YSYX) SoC
///

In fact, the FPGA SoM is already inserted on the back of the board in advance, **so students do not need to install it themselves**. In addition, a crystal oscillator must be inserted into the SoC board for it to work correctly; **this crystal oscillator has also been pre-inserted into the crystal oscillator socket on the board**, and students only need to confirm it.

!!! info "Installing/removing the FPGA SoM and crystal oscillator"
    The FPGA SoM supplied with this SoC is a [SoM (System on Module)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/System_on_module) using a SODIMM 204P interface design **(DDR3 standard-compatible interface)**. This type of interface is commonly used for laptop memory modules. By default, the FPGA SoM is already inserted into the slot, and **generally does not need to be removed**. If removal is really necessary, students must do it themselves; the specific method is as follows:

    ![](../../../res/img/brd/starry-sky-l/v1.2/fpga/sodimm_black_1.webp)
    /// caption
    Installing the FPGA SoM
    ///

    When installing the FPGA SoM, insert it into the slot at an **angle of about 30 degrees**, **and make sure that all gold fingers are inserted to the same depth**. Then hold both sides of the FPGA SoM with both hands and press down simultaneously until both sides are pressed under the **retaining clips** of the slot and you hear a "click". To remove the SoM, do the reverse: gently push the retaining clips outward, the FPGA SoM will pop up automatically, and then pull the FPGA SoM out.

    Installing and removing the crystal oscillator is easier than the FPGA SoM. Just take a 25 MHz crystal oscillator out of the **rigid anti-static bag** and insert it into the board's crystal oscillator socket in the correct orientation. Because the crystal oscillator pins are longer than the depth of the socket slots, **there is no need to insert the crystal oscillator all the way in**; when you find that you can no longer push it in with moderate force, stop. The correct orientation and depth are shown in the figures below:

    ![](../../../res/img/brd/starry-sky-l/v1.2/perip/osc_1_1.webp)
    /// caption
    Correct orientation of the crystal oscillator
    ///

    ![](../../../res/img/brd/starry-sky-l/v1.2/perip/osc_2.webp)
    /// caption
    Correct insertion depth of the crystal oscillator
    ///

!!! warning
    - Do not insert the crystal oscillator in the wrong orientation; **students need to confirm this again before powering on**.
    - You can remove and insert the crystal oscillator directly by hand, but be careful not to touch the pins of the crystal oscillator with your hands.
    - You can hold the metal case of the crystal oscillator to insert or remove it.

After the crystal oscillator is correctly inserted, the **micro DIP switches** on the hardware still need to be used to correctly set the working state of the SoC chip. Specifically, two steps are required:

- Enable the PLL output and select the frequency of the PLL output core clock
- Select the processor core for a specific student ID

During the back-end integration of the Phase III SoC, the project team assigned an index to each student's core, and this index can be used to select your own core. First, open the [Phase III processor core index and student ID mapping table (student-id.md)](https://github.com/maksyuki/StarrySky-res/blob/main/software/v1p2/student-id.md), **and then look up your core index according to your student ID**. For example, the core with student ID `ysyx_210000` has core index `1`.

Now let us explain the function definition of each position of the **micro DIP switches**. There are two micro DIP switches on the board. The left DIP switch has 4 positions and is used to **set the clock output state**. The right DIP switch has 6 positions and is used to **set the core selection state**:

![](../../../res/img/brd/starry-sky-l/v1.2/perip/dip_switch_1.webp)
/// caption
DIP switch position function definitions
///

4-position DIP switch function definitions **(from left to right)**:

- PLL clock enable bit **(1 bit)**
- PLL output clock frequency selection bits **(3 bits)**

6-position DIP switch function definitions **(from left to right)**:

- Undefined **(1 bit)**
- Processor core selection bits **(5 bits)**

!!! warning "Core selection DIP definition"
    The highest bit of the 6-position DIP switch **(the first one from the left)** has no defined function.

DIP switch setting is divided into two parts. The first part is setting the PLL clock enable bit and the PLL output core clock frequency. We recommend **first setting the PLL output core clock frequency to 25 MHz and starting testing from a low frequency**. After your core can run the test program we provide at a 25 MHz core clock, you can then try to gradually increase the processor core clock frequency. Now let us explain the correspondence between the DIP switch positions and the signals on the SoC. The RCG **(global clock and reset module)** of the Phase III SoC is shown in the figure below:

![](../../../res/img/brd/starry-sky-l/v1.2/core/rcg_intro.webp)
/// caption
RCG module of the Phase III SoC
///

The **PLL clock enable bit** of the DIP switch corresponds to `clk_sel` in the red box in the upper-left corner of the figure above. When `clk_sel=1`, the processor core clock `clk_core` uses the PLL output clock, and the **PLL clock enable bit** on the DIP switch needs to be set to `ON`. When `clk_sel=0`, `clk_core` directly uses the external active crystal oscillator clock `sys_clk`, and the **PLL clock enable bit** on the DIP switch needs to be set to `OFF`.

In addition, the **PLL output clock frequency selection bits** correspond to `pll_cfg[2:0]` in the red box in the upper-left corner of the figure above, and are used to set the frequency of the PLL output clock, that is, the processor core clock frequency. The **correspondence between DIP switch positions and PLL output core clock frequency** is shown in the table below:

| PLL output clock frequency selection bits (0 = OFF, 1 = ON) | Processor core clock frequency |
| :-: | :-: |
| 3'b000 | 25MHz  |
| 3'b001 | 50MHz  |
| 3'b010 | 100MHz |
| 3'b011 | 150MHz |
| 3'b100 | 200MHz |
| 3'b101 | 250MHz |
| 3'b110 | 300MHz |
| 3'b111 | 350MHz |

For example, when the PLL output bits are set to `OFF-OFF-OFF`, the PLL output core clock frequency is 25 MHz. In fact, the clock setting in the ***DIP switch position function definitions*** figure happens to be 25 MHz.

!!! tip "Clock DIP switch setting"
    The lowest bit of the **PLL output clock frequency selection bits** in the ***DIP switch position and core clock frequency correspondence table*** above corresponds to position 4 of the DIP switch on the board. Therefore, to set the PLL output clock frequency to 200 MHz, set the PLL output clock selection bits to `ON-OFF-OFF` **(corresponding to positions 2, 3, and 4 of the DIP switch)**, as shown in the figure below:

    ![](../../../res/img/brd/starry-sky-l/v1.2/perip/dip_switch_2.webp)
    /// caption
    DIP switch setting to enable the PLL and multiply the clock to 200 MHz
    ///

!!! tip "Tips"
    - If the DIP switch has a **yellow dust-proof film** on it, peel it off before toggling.
    - The mechanical DIP switches on the board are micro DIP switches with a 1.27 mm pitch. Each position is rather small and difficult to toggle directly by hand; you can use tweezers, a paper clip, or another pointed object. **Please use pointed objects safely**.
    - To make it convenient to measure the processor core clock frequency, the board provides a **core clock divided-by-4 test point** and an **external crystal oscillator clock test point**:

    ![](../../../res/img/brd/starry-sky-l/v1.2/perip/osc_tp.webp)
    /// caption
    Clock frequency test points
    ///

    The **external crystal oscillator clock test point** is used to test whether the external active crystal oscillator oscillates correctly, while the **core clock divided-by-4 test point** is used to measure the core clock frequency. Students with an oscilloscope can use this test point to measure the PLL core clock output under different DIP switch settings. **Note, however, that the measured value is one quarter of the PLL core clock**.

The second part of DIP switch setting is to select your own processor core. One Student One Chip Phase III integrates multiple students' cores into one SoC and uses the high/low level of an external signal line to select different cores. This external signal line is implemented by the **processor core selection bits** on the DIP switch, and the **processor core selection bits** correspond to `core_dip[4:0]` in the red box on the left side of the Phase III SoC architecture diagram below:

![](../../../res/img/brd/starry-sky-l/v1.2/core/soc_intro.webp)
/// caption
Phase III SoC architecture diagram
///

![](../../../res/img/brd/starry-sky-l/v1.2/perip/dip_switch_3.webp)

Because of **positive logic**, the corresponding DIP switch settings from left to right are `OFF-OFF-OFF-OFF-ON`. To select the core with index `6`, the corresponding code is `5'b00110`, and the DIP switch settings from left to right are `OFF-OFF-ON-ON-OFF`.

!!! danger "Toggling DIP switches"
    - Like slide switches, DIP switches must be properly toggled to one side rather than the middle position **(mechanical dead zone)** before powering on, to prevent the SoC from sampling an unstable level on the DIP switch state.
    - Do not toggle DIP switches while powered. Doing so may cause large inrush currents in the board's power network, affecting the board's operating stability and possibly even burning out the board.

### Hardware Design

This section describes the hardware design of StarrySky v1.2 in detail. First, a brief summary of the main hardware design features of the v1.2 board:

- Designed with Cadence Orcad/Allegro **(three weeks)**, using a **six-layer** stack-up **(TOP-GND02-ART03-PWR04-GND05-BOT)** and ENIG surface finish, and passing four-wire low-resistance testing.
- Board length matching: ChipLink traces are length-matched within the group to **0/50 mil** referenced to **tx_clk** and **rx_clk** respectively; the two SDIO interfaces are length-matched within the group to **0/30 mil** referenced to clk; USB2.0 is length-matched within the group to **0/50 mil** referenced to clk; the 5 pairs of USB differential signals are matched within each pair to **0/25 mil**.
- Ground vias are placed on both sides of the IPEX antenna of the SDIO WiFi at **30 mil** spacing, and the pads of the power inductor are **copper-cleared (keep-out)**.
- VGA and all crystal oscillator clock outputs are **surrounded by ground**, kept away from high-frequency and analog signals, and all reference a complete ground.
- The analog ground is **single-point isolated**; each chip has complete power filtering; the power network uses copper pours for connections to ensure stable power supply, and a sufficient number of **return ground vias** are placed.

#### Power Network

The power network topology of the SoC board is shown in the figure below:

![](../../../res/img/brd/starry-sky-l/v1.2/core/power_tp.webp)
/// caption
Board power network topology
///

As can be seen from the figure above, the entire board is powered uniformly from a 5 V supply. To flexibly control the power supply of peripherals, the board is designed with three power inputs:

- The 5 V power from the SoC's USB serial port
- The 5 V power from the FPGA PS-side USB serial port
- The 5 V DC power adapter supply from the external USB interface

The board uses the **power selection switch** to select one of the three inputs to supply the board. After passing through the **latching power switch**, the 5 V supply is stepped down by three LDO chips to 3.3 V, 1.8 V, and 1.2 V respectively for the various peripherals of the SoC and FPGA. The schematic is as follows:

![](../../../res/img/brd/starry-sky-l/v1.2/sch/sch_pwr_sck.webp)
/// caption
Power input interfaces
///

Here, USB_PWR, PS_PWR, and DC_PWR are three standard Type-C receptacles. USB_PWR exposes the power net **`5V_VBUS1`** and the differential input signals **`MAIN_DP/DN`** required by the SoC serial port; PS_PWR exposes the power net **`5V_VBUS2`** and the differential input signals **`PS_UART_DP/DN`** required by the FPGA PS-side serial port. Because DC_PWR is only used for power supply, only the power net **`5V_DC`** is exposed on its connector.

!!! info "Purpose of the 5.1K pull-down resistor on the CC pin of the Type-C receptacle"
    The CC pin on Type-C stands for [Configuration Channel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USB-C) and is used to detect cable insertion, identify cable orientation, configure the operating mode **(current, PD mode)**, and negotiate the establishment of DFP **(downstream-facing port, host side)** and UFP **(upstream-facing port, device side)** roles. Relative to the PC host, the board's Type-C interface acts as a device-side interface, that is, it has the UFP role. The protocol specifies that both the **`CC1`** and **`CC2`** pins of a UFP require a pull-down resistor **`Rd=5.1KΩ`**.

The three power inputs described above pass through two [single-pole double-throw (SPDT) slide switches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Switch#Contact_terminology), PWR-SEL1 and PWR-SEL2. By combining the positions of these two switches, one power input is selected to feed the **latching power switch PWR-LOCK**. PWR-LOCK is a [double-pole double-throw (DPDT) slide switch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Switch#Contact_terminology), and only one side of its switching contacts is used here. When the latching switch is not locked down, it connects pins **`2`** and **`1`**, and the power net is not connected. When the latching power switch is pressed down and locked, PWR-LOCK connects its **`2`** and **`3`** pins, so that one power input passes through the latching switch into the downstream power network. The power then passes through two protection devices, the bidirectional TVS diode SMAJ5.0CA and a 2 A fuse, and is filtered by multiple capacitors, finally producing a stable 5 V digital power rail **`5V_IN`**:

![](../../../res/img/brd/starry-sky-l/v1.2/sch/sch_pwr_sel.webp)
/// caption
Power selection switches
///

After that, **`5V_IN`** enters three RT2515H LDO chips and is stepped down to 3.3 V, 1.8 V, and 1.2 V. The RT2515H is a high-performance voltage regulator supporting a minimum input-output dropout of 200 mV and a maximum output current of 2 A. Different output voltages can be produced by configuring the resistor values on its **`ADJ`** pin. In addition, the 3.3 V power net **`VCC_3V3`** is connected to a red LED to indicate whether the power circuit is working properly:

![](../../../res/img/brd/starry-sky-l/v1.2/sch/sch_pwr_ldo.webp)
/// caption
Power conversion circuit
///

#### SoC Onboard Programmer (HFPLink) {#soc-hfplink}

The SoC part of the board carries a NOR Flash programmer named **HFPLink**. **HFPLink** is actually a Flash programmer designed by the project team. **HFP** in the name stands for **High-speed QSPI-Flash Programmer**, and because it supports drag-and-drop programming just like **DAPlink**, the suffix **Link** was added following that naming convention, resulting in the name **HFPLink**. #### SoC Function Selection Switch {#soc_2}

The function selection switch in the SoC circuit is designed to allow the SoC's USB-to-serial and HFPLink to share one Type-C interface and to switch the working state of the NOR Flash synchronously. This function is controlled by an SPDT slide switch named HFP-SW. When HFP-SW is toggled so that its **`1`** and **`2`** pins are connected, the **`PROG_SW_SEL`** net is low; then pin **`6`** of all analog switches WAS3157B is low, which connects the signal nets on pins **`1`** and **`4`** of each analog switch. When HFP-SW is toggled so that its **`3`** and **`2`** pins are connected, the **`PROG_SW_SEL`** net is high; then pin **`6`** of all analog switches WAS3157B is high, which connects the signal nets on pins **`3`** and **`4`** of each analog switch:

![](../../../res/img/brd/starry-sky-l/v1.2/sch/sch_soc_func_sw.webp)
/// caption
SoC function selection switch
///

This circuit can be designed this way because **the HFPLink and the SoC's USB-to-serial do not work at the same time**, so the differential signal **`MAIN_DP/DN`** on one Type-C interface can be shared. Moreover, when HFPLink flashes the NOR Flash, the SPI signal lines connecting the SoC to the NOR Flash need to be disconnected; conversely, when the SoC needs to fetch instructions from the NOR Flash and execute programs, the SPI signal lines connecting HFPLink to the NOR Flash also need to be disconnected. In other words, the connection states of 6 signal lines in total (2 differential + 4 SPI) need to be **switched synchronously** between the two states of HFPLink working and the SoC working normally.

#### PL VGA

On the FPGA PL side, the board carries a standard VGA interface that can be used to display images or video. The color mode of the data signal lines is RGB444, and the DAC for each RGB data channel is implemented with a **resistor network**. The schematic for this part is shown below:

![](../../../res/img/brd/starry-sky-l/v1.2/sch/sch_pl_vga.webp)
/// caption
PL VGA

!!! info "Resistor network value calculation"
    The VGA standard specifies that the signals on the `VGA_R`, `VGA_G`, and `VGA_B` pins are analog, ranging from `0~0.714V`. A voltage of `0V` on each analog signal represents no color, and `0.714V` represents full color. Because RGB444 is used, each channel has 2^4=16 color levels. For example, for the `VGA_R` channel, let the combined input signal VGA_R3~VGA_R0 be $VGA_R[3:0]$, and let the voltage of the `VGA_R` net be $V_{R}$. To display the `VGA_R` channel color correctly, it is sufficient to satisfy:

    - When $VGA_R[3:0]=4'b0000$, $V_{R}=0V$
    - When $VGA_R[3:0]=4'b1111$, $V_{R}<=0.714V$
    - When $VGA_R[3:0]$ takes any value in $[4'b0000, 4'b1111]$, for any two values $VGA_R[3:0]_1 <= VGA_R[3:0]_2$, $V_{R1}<=V_{R2}$ is satisfied

    It follows that the resistor weights on VGA_R3~VGA_R0 are different. The specific resistance values can be calculated with circuit simulation software, such as the free [MultisimLive](https://www.multisim.com/):

    ![](../../../res/img/brd/starry-sky-l/v1.2/sch/vga_dac_sim.webp)
    /// caption
    MultisimLive simulation of the VGA resistor network
    ///

    The free version of **MultisimLive** has limits on circuit size, but it is entirely sufficient for the task of calculating resistor network values here.

!!! info "Why the Xilinx ZYNQ series was chosen for the FPGA"
    - There are more Xilinx ZYNQ series development boards on the market than other FPGA models, so more software and hardware reference materials are available.
    - The PS-side peripheral controllers of ZYNQ can be routed to PL-side IO through EMIO, making it convenient to compare and verify against IP functions developed by the project team.
    - The FPGA SoM carried by this version of the board not only provides the necessary ChipLink memory access support for the SoC, but also **provides board support for FPGA prototyping of the next-generation SoC**. Achieving this goal requires FPGA devices with efficient and stable software development tools and IP component support. When selecting the FPGA, the project team also tried several domestic FPGAs, but their software development environments still have much room for improvement compared with Xilinx's **Vivado+Vitis**, so the project team ultimately chose Xilinx ZYNQ series FPGA devices.

#### ChipLink Hardware System

!!! info "ChipLink programming"
    Before the FPGA SoM was shipped to students, the ChipLink hardware system described in this section had already been programmed into the FPGA's onboard Flash, so students can use it directly without compiling and downloading it themselves.

ChipLink is an inter-chip low-speed bus communication protocol **(between the FPGA and the SoC)** proposed by SiFive. It is mainly used to split and reassemble the AXI4 requests sent by the SoC in order to reliably access hardware logic resources on the FPGA. The Verilog code of the ChipLink controller on this FPGA SoM was generated by Chisel and passed simulation on VCS. In the actual implementation, the `ChiplinkTop` and all its submodules used in the VCS simulation were imported into one file and renamed `ChiplinkTop.v`. Because many signals inside the `ChiplinkTop` instantiated during VCS simulation are dangling or tied to constants, the project team wrapped another layer, `chiplink_ctrl`, on top of `ChiplinkTop`, instantiated `ChiplinkTop` in this module and tied the corresponding ports to constants, and finally named this module `chiplink_ctrl.v`. In addition, because the DDR address mapping of ZYNQ differs from the address range accessed by the SoC, the access addresses need to be remapped. The ZYNQ memory address map is shown below:

![](../../../res/img/brd/starry-sky-l/v1.2/fpga/chiplink_addr_map.webp)
/// caption
ZYNQ OCM/DDR address mapping
///

OCM stands for **On-Chip Memory**, which is built into the ZYNQ chip and can serve as low-latency shared memory between the dual-core CPUs on the PS side. OCM is also the memory where the dual-core CPUs execute apps, **so it is necessary to ensure that the access addresses of the Phase III SoC are correctly mapped to the DDR space; otherwise, once remapped to the OCM space, the programs running on the PS side may be overwritten**. As can be seen from the figure above, ZYNQ supports a total of 1 GB of address range from `0x0010_0000` to `0x3FFF_FFF`, while the ZYNQ SoM we provide has only 512 MB of onboard DDR3. To simplify the implementation, we **remap** the access requests of the Phase III SoC starting at `0x8000_0000` to `0x0010_0000`, so that as long as the addresses accessed by the PS-side CPU when it needs to access DDR do not overlap with the SoC's access addresses, it is fine. The remapping code is as follows:

```verilog
assign io_axi4_0_awaddr = aw_addr - 32'h8000_0000 + 32'h0010_0000;
assign io_axi4_0_araddr = ar_addr - 32'h8000_0000 + 32'h0010_0000;
assign chiplink_cpu_int = 1'b0;
```

Then, adding `chiplink_ctrl` to the **Block Design** and connecting it to the ZYNQ system completes the hardware design. The final hardware system is shown below:

![](../../../res/img/brd/starry-sky-l/v1.2/fpga/chiplink_bd.webp)
/// caption
ChipLink hardware system Block Design
///

Here, the **ZYNQ Processing System** enables the AXI HP slave interface, the `FCLK_CLK0` and `FCLK_CLK1` clock outputs, and the `FCLK_RESET0_N` reset output. `FCLK_CLK0` is set to a clock frequency of 25 MHz and is used as the input clock of `chiplink_ctrl`. `FCLK_CLK1` is set to a clock frequency of 100 MHz and is used by the **AXI Interconnect** for AXI master communication. The other two clocks are connected to their respective **Processor System Reset** blocks to generate reset signals. Finally, an ILA core is added to sample the AXI4 signals of `chiplink_ctrl`.

After that, the pins of the `chiplink_ctrl` module also need to be constrained. Because the `chiplink_ctrl` module has many pins, they need to be carefully checked and confirmed. In addition, the Phase III SoC uses a 110 nm process, so the pin level standard needs to be constrained to LVCMOS33. Finally, a clock constraint needs to be applied to the `chiplink_ctrl` pins. The specific constraints are shown below:

![](../../../res/img/brd/starry-sky-l/v1.2/fpga/chiplink_contr.webp)
/// caption
ChipLink timing constraints
///

Then use Vivado for synthesis and implementation. After implementation, Vivado reported 3 timing-related Critical Warnings. After consulting Xilinx's **UG906** manual and analyzing the design code, the project team believes these three warnings are mainly because **the clock source generated at the PS-side MCM/PLL cannot have a common path defined on the PL side**, which does not affect the design and can be ignored.

![](../../../res/img/brd/starry-sky-l/v1.2/fpga/chiplink_log.webp)
/// caption
